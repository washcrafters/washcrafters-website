How Much Does Snow Removal Cost in Halifax? (2026–27 Price Guide)

By Kendall Scott September 2, 2026 8 min read

Quick answer

Snow removal in Halifax, NS typically costs $79–$180 per visit for on-call driveway clearing, or $700–$2,600 per season for a flat-rate contract that covers every storm from November through April. Most homeowners land in the $900–$1,700 range for a seasonal plan that covers the driveway plus a walkway and salting — full-service plans with no snowfall minimum and priority response sit at the top of that range.

If you're searching "how much does snow removal cost" before the first real storm hits, here's what to actually budget for in Halifax this winter — and why the answer depends as much on how you pay as on how big your driveway is.

What Affects the Cost of Snow Removal?

  • Driveway size and layout. More surface area, a slope, or an awkward turnaround all mean more time on site — the main driver of price.
  • Per-visit vs. seasonal contract. A flat seasonal rate spreads the cost evenly regardless of how many storms roll through; per-visit pricing can win in a mild winter and lose badly in a rough one.
  • What's included. Driveway-only is the cheapest tier. Adding the walkway, steps, and salt/ice-melt typically adds a few hundred dollars to a seasonal plan.
  • Snowfall trigger. Most plans clear at 5 cm or more. A "no minimum, every snowfall" plan costs more because it responds to light dustings and freezing rain too.
  • Equipment used. A plow truck clears a driveway fast but can gouge asphalt or interlock edges and leaves a packed wall of snow behind; a snowblower-and-shovel crew is gentler on residential surfaces and can get closer to doors and walkways, which some companies (including us, in West Bedford) price as the standard rather than an upgrade.
  • Response time. A guarantee that your driveway is clear before your morning commute costs more than standard "we'll get to it within a day" service.
  • Accessibility. Long driveways, hills, tight side-yard access, and having nowhere left to pile snow after a big winter all affect the quote.

What's Included in Professional Snow Removal

A proper snow-removal visit clears the driveway down to pavement, including the windrow — the ridge of packed snow the city plow leaves at the end of your driveway, which is often the part homeowners find most frustrating to deal with themselves. Walkway and steps are cleared too, if your plan includes them, along with ice-melt application where people actually walk. Ask what equipment a company uses: it's a fair question, and the answer affects both the price and what's left on your lawn and driveway edges afterward.

"Snow removal" doesn't always mean the same thing

Two quotes that look similar on price can cover very different work. Some plans are driveway-only with a 5 cm trigger; others clear every snowfall and include the walkway, steps, and proactive salting before freezing rain. Before comparing prices between companies, confirm exactly what's covered, what triggers a visit, and whether salt/ice-melt is included or billed separately.

DIY vs. Professional Snow Removal: Cost Comparison

A snowblower is the obvious alternative to hiring it out, and for some homeowners it's the right call — but the math is closer than it looks:

  • A decent snowblower runs $600–$2,000+ upfront, plus gas, maintenance, and somewhere to store it in the off-season.
  • A single storm can take 30–60+ minutes of your own time, and in a busy HRM winter that repeats several times a week — often before you need to leave for work.
  • Shovelling wet, heavy snow is physically demanding and a well-known source of back injuries and cardiac strain, especially first thing on a cold morning.
  • If you travel, work irregular hours, or just sleep through an overnight storm, a missed clearing can mean an icy, unsafe driveway by the time you're back.

For a small, flat driveway and a homeowner who doesn't mind the work, a snowblower can pay for itself over a few winters. For anyone who'd rather not be out at 6 a.m. with a shovel every time it snows, the $700–$2,600 seasonal range above buys back that time and the guesswork of when to go out.

Is Professional Snow Removal Worth the Cost?

Beyond convenience, there's a real liability angle: many Halifax homeowners are responsible for keeping the sidewalk in front of their property clear, and an un-shovelled walkway or an icy driveway is a genuine slip-and-fall risk for visitors, delivery drivers, and your own family. A cleared driveway also means you're not stuck at home — or late — the morning after a storm.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does snow removal cost in Halifax?

Snow removal in Halifax, NS typically costs $79–$180 per visit for on-call driveway clearing, or $700–$2,600 per season for a flat-rate contract covering every storm from November through April. Most homeowners land in the $900–$1,700 range for a seasonal plan that covers the driveway plus a walkway and salting. Full-service plans with no snowfall minimum and priority response sit at the top of that range.

Is a seasonal contract cheaper than paying per visit?

It depends on the winter. At $79–$180 per visit, a mild winter with 8–10 storms can cost less pay-per-visit than a seasonal plan over $1,000. A heavy winter with 15–20+ storms usually favours the seasonal rate, since it's a flat price no matter how many times it snows. Seasonal plans also guarantee you a spot on the route, which fills up fast once the snow starts.

Does snow removal include salt or ice melt?

Not always. Driveway-only plans are usually just plowing or shovelling. Salting and ice-melt application on the walkway and steps is typically a separate line item or an upgraded tier, so confirm exactly what's included before comparing prices between quotes.

How much snow needs to fall before a company comes?

Most seasonal plans trigger at 5 cm (about 2 inches) of accumulation. Premium or full-service tiers often remove that minimum and clear every snowfall, including light dustings and freezing rain, for a higher price.

Do I still need to shovel anything myself?

If your plan covers the driveway only, yes — you'd still handle the walkway, steps and any salting yourself unless you upgrade to a plan that includes them.
