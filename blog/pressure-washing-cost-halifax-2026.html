How Much Does Pressure Washing Cost in Halifax? (2026 Price Guide)

By Kendall Scott August 28, 2026 7 min read

Quick answer

Professional pressure washing in Halifax, NS typically costs $0.25–$0.40 per square foot for concrete surfaces like driveways and walkways in 2026. A typical 2-car driveway (around 600 sq ft) runs about $150–$240. Stone, paver, and brick surfaces run higher — $0.40–$0.60 per square foot — because their textured, more porous surface takes more time and lower pressure to clean properly.

If you've searched "how much does pressure washing cost" because your driveway has gone from grey to rust-streaked, mossy, or just generally grim, you're in the right place. Pressure washing is one of the fastest, most affordable ways to take years off the look of your concrete, stone, or brick — here's exactly what to budget for in 2026, and what actually moves the price up or down.

What Affects the Cost of Pressure Washing?

  • Surface size. The single biggest factor — more square footage means more time on site.
  • Surface material. Plain concrete and asphalt handle higher pressure and clean faster than textured, porous stone, pavers, or brick.
  • Staining type. Rust, oil, tire marks, and iron staining from sprinklers or well water often need a pre-treatment pass before washing.
  • Buildup and algae. A driveway that hasn't been cleaned in years takes longer than one that's washed annually.
  • Accessibility. Long driveways, tight side-yard access, or parked cars and planters that need to be worked around all add time.

Concrete vs. Stone, Paver & Brick: Why the Price Differs

Not every hard surface gets cleaned the same way, which is why stone, paver, and brick work is priced higher than plain concrete. Pavers have sand-filled joints that can be blown out by too much pressure, and natural stone and brick are more porous and textured than poured concrete — both need lower pressure and more dwell time to clean safely without damage. Plain concrete and asphalt are denser and more uniform, so they clean faster at higher pressure.

Efflorescence and grime on brick and stone respond well to pressure washing — it just takes a gentler touch than a plain concrete slab.

A note on pricing: the stone/paver/brick rate above is a general range based on how these surfaces are typically priced relative to concrete. Every property is different, and features like pillars, retaining walls, or small accent areas are usually bundled into a driveway or patio job rather than priced on their own. A free on-site quote will always give you an exact number.

What's Included in Professional Pressure Washing

At WashCrafters, a standard pressure washing job includes a pre-treatment pass on visible stains, a full wash using the right pressure and nozzle for the surface (a rotary surface cleaner for flat concrete, a wand for edges and detail work), and a rinse that clears debris away rather than leaving it pooled on your lawn or in your storm drain.

Pressure washing isn't the right tool for every surface

Concrete, stone, and brick can handle real pressure. Vinyl siding, asphalt shingles, and most decking can't — high pressure on those surfaces risks cracking, stripping granules, or forcing water behind panels. That's why we use soft washing (low pressure plus a cleaning solution) for siding, roofs, and decks, and save pressure washing for hard, non-porous surfaces like driveways and patios. See our soft washing vs. pressure washing guide for the full breakdown of when each method applies.

DIY vs. Professional Pressure Washing: Cost Comparison

A consumer-grade electric pressure washer runs $150–$400 to buy, or $50–$80/day to rent, which looks like an obvious way to save money on a one-time driveway clean. In practice, the math is less clear-cut:

  • Consumer pressure washers max out at 1,300–2,000 PSI, well below what's needed to lift embedded rust and oil stains — results are often noticeably lighter than a professional-grade wash.
  • A rented pressure washer used with the wrong nozzle or held too close can etch concrete, blow sand out of paver joints, or leave visible stripe marks that don't fade.
  • DIY driveway cleaning is a half-day project without a surface cleaner attachment, versus a professional visit that's usually done in under two hours.

For a small, lightly soiled pad, DIY can be a reasonable choice. For a full driveway, stone or paver surfaces, or anything with real staining, the $0.25–$0.60 per square foot professional cost is usually the better value once your own time and the risk of visible damage are factored in.

Is Pressure Washing Worth the Cost?

For most Halifax homeowners, yes. A stained, algae-covered driveway is one of the first things anyone notices about a property, and pressure washing is one of the cheapest ways to meaningfully improve curb appeal — especially compared to resurfacing or replacing concrete, which typically runs into the thousands. Regular cleaning also helps concrete and masonry last longer by removing organic growth and staining before it has a chance to etch or discolour the surface permanently.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does pressure washing cost in Halifax?

Concrete surfaces like driveways and walkways run $0.25–$0.40 per square foot, so a typical 2-car driveway (around 600 sq ft) costs about $150–$240. Stone, paver, and brick surfaces run higher, $0.40–$0.60 per square foot, because their textured, more porous surface needs lower pressure and more time to clean properly.

What's the difference between pressure washing and soft washing?

Pressure washing uses high water pressure alone and is built for hard, non-porous surfaces like concrete, stone, and brick. Soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution and is what we use on siding, roofs, and decks, where high pressure risks cracking, stripping, or forcing water behind the surface. We match the method to the surface rather than pressure washing everything.

Does pressure washing damage concrete or pavers?

Not when it's done correctly. Concrete and dense natural stone handle higher pressure safely, though we still use a surface cleaner attachment and controlled PSI to avoid etching or leaving stripe marks. Pavers and softer stone need lower pressure and more dwell time, which is part of why they're priced differently from plain concrete.

How often should I get my driveway pressure washed?

Once a year is typical for most Halifax driveways, especially with winter road salt, sand, and iron staining from sprinklers or well water. Shaded or tree-covered driveways that grow algae faster often benefit from cleaning every 8–10 months instead.

Do I need to be home during a pressure washing appointment?

No. As long as we have clear access to the area and an outdoor water source, most appointments don't require you to be home. We'll confirm access details with you when your quote is scheduled.
