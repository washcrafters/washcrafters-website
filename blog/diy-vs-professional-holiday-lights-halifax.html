DIY vs. Professional Holiday Lights in Halifax: What It Really Costs

By Kendall Scott November 20, 2026 7 min read

Quick answer

DIY holiday lights usually look cheaper up front and cost more than they seem once you count the ladder time, extra strands, cords and clips, mid-season fixes and storage. On a small single-storey home with a simple roofline, DIY can be a good weekend job. On a two-storey home, a steep or complex roofline, or if you'd rather not be on a frosty ladder, a professional install starts at $649 and covers the lights, installation, service calls, takedown and storage.

Every November the same debate plays out in driveways around the HRM: pick up a few boxes of lights and do it yourself, or hire someone? There's no answer that's right for everyone, and we'd rather you make the right call than the one that's good for us. Here's an honest look at what each option really involves, including the situations where we'd tell you to do it yourself.

What You're Really Signing Up For With DIY

The box says "just hang and plug in." The job is a bit longer than that:

  • Measuring your roofline and working out how much light you need
  • Buying lights, clips, outdoor-rated cords and a timer, and making sure the bulbs and colours match
  • Testing every strand before it goes up, since finding a dead one on the roof is a bad time
  • Hanging everything, at height, in November and December weather
  • Fixing whatever goes dark mid-season
  • Taking it all down in January, then wrapping, sorting and storing it

None of that is hard, exactly. It's just more than most people picture when they're standing in the store aisle.

The Ladder Problem

This is the one we'd want you to take seriously. Ladder work at the roofline is one of the most common sources of serious falls around a home, and the conditions for holiday lights are close to the worst: cold hands, wet or frosty surfaces, wind, and daylight that's gone by late afternoon. It's not a risk worth taking for a decoration.

If you do DIY, keep it safe

Stick to areas you can reach safely from the ground or a short ladder. Have someone steady the ladder, never stand on the top rungs, and skip any day with wind, ice or frost on the surfaces. If you'd have to lean, stretch or climb onto the roof to reach it, that part is a job for a professional.

Why DIY Often Looks Different

A professional display looks clean because every run is built to the house, so the lights start and finish where your roofline does. With DIY, a strand that's slightly too short leaves a gap, and one that's too long gets bunched or draped somewhere. Mixed boxes and brands can also differ in colour, and "warm white" isn't the same from one maker to the next. Cords that show and bulbs that aren't evenly spaced are the details people notice from the street.

A professional install: every run is cut to fit the roofline, with evenly spaced bulbs.

What Happens When a Section Goes Out

Wind, ice and the odd squirrel happen to every display. With DIY, finding the bad bulb or loose clip is your job, usually after dark and usually on the highest part of the house. Our installs include free service calls all season, so you text us and we come put it right.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to put up holiday lights yourself?

Usually it looks cheaper up front, because you only pay for the lights. But you also need clips, outdoor-rated cords, a timer and a ladder, plus your own time to measure, test, hang, fix and take everything down. Strands come in fixed lengths, so you may buy more than you need, and a dead section is your job to track down. A professional install starts at $649 and includes all of that.

Is it safe to hang holiday lights yourself?

On a single-storey home you can reach safely from the ground or a short ladder, it can be. Ladder work at the roofline is one of the most common sources of serious falls around a home, and frost, wet shingles and early darkness make it worse. If you have to climb above your comfort level, or the roof is steep or two storeys up, that's the point to hire it out.

How long does it take to install holiday lights?

A professional crew usually does it in a single visit, and most homes take a few hours. For DIY, it varies a lot with your roofline, but plan for time to measure, test the strands, hang them, and then do it all again in reverse at takedown.

What does a professional holiday lighting install include?

Ours includes a design consult, commercial-grade LED lights cut to fit your home, clips and an outdoor timer, installation with hidden cords, free service calls all season, January takedown and off-season storage. We're fully insured and our crew is ladder-trained.

Do I have to buy the lights if I hire you?

No. The lights, clips and timer stay with WashCrafters, and we store them for you between seasons. You're paying for the design, installation, service and takedown, not for a set of lights you then have to find room for.
